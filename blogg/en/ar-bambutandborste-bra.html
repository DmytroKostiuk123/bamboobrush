Are bamboo toothbrushes good? Pros and cons

We sell bamboo toothbrushes, but we intend to be honest. Here are the pros and cons of bamboo compared with plastic, without exaggeration.

Contents

The advantages of bamboo

  • A plastic-free handle. Bamboo is a fast-growing grass that needs no plastic, oil or glue. The handle is fully biodegradable.
  • Compostable. A bamboo handle breaks down in compost, while a plastic toothbrush never breaks down but turns into microplastics.
  • No microplastics from the handle. Since it isn't plastic, it doesn't shed plastic particles as it wears.
  • A renewable material. Bamboo grows quickly and needs little artificial irrigation or pesticides.

The disadvantages – and the nuances

A good guide mentions the downsides too:

  • The bristles are the tricky part. Many "bamboo toothbrushes" have a bamboo handle but nylon bristles – and nylon is plastic. Always check what the bristles are made of. Ours are natural bristle (boar bristle), a protein called keratin, not plastic.
  • Natural bristles absorb water. Keratin bristles can wear slightly faster than nylon, but in practice it isn't a problem and you barely notice. The bristles do get a little softer when wet, though.
  • Antibacterial, but moisture matters. Bamboo contains some antibacterial substances, which is a plus. But bacteria and mould like moisture, so rinse the brush and let it air-dry standing up between uses. That keeps it fresh.
  • The handle makes no difference to your enamel. How much a toothbrush wears your teeth depends on the toothpaste, how hard you brush and how soft the bristles are. The bristles are what count: wet natural bristles soften to below 1 GPa, softer than nylon (2–3 GPa even when wet) – so they are gentler on the enamel.

In short: the real strength of a bamboo toothbrush is the environment – plastic-free and compostable – not that it cleans better or is gentler on your teeth. Cleaning is just as good as with an ordinary brush of the same softness.

Who is a bamboo toothbrush for?

It suits you if you want to cut down on plastic in everyday life without compromising on oral hygiene. You brush exactly as usual – the difference shows in your rubbish bin, not in your mouth.
