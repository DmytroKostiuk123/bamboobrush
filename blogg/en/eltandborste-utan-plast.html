Is there an electric toothbrush without plastic?

Short answer: no, not the whole thing. The handle can't be made plastic-free. But the part you throw away several times a year, the brush head, can be swapped for one made of bamboo with natural bristles.

Contents

Why the handle can't be plastic-free

An electric toothbrush has a battery, a motor and a circuit board in the handle. All of that has to sit inside a sealed casing that can handle water every day. The casing is almost always plastic, sometimes metal with seals and buttons made of plastic or rubber. We don't know of any electric toothbrush with a handle that is completely free of plastic.

That matters less than you might think. A handle lasts many years if you charge it properly. It isn't where the plastic piles up.

The brush head is what becomes rubbish

An ordinary brush head is plastic through and through: a plastic stem, nylon bristles and often a small metal part inside. If you replace it every three months, as most manufacturers recommend, that's four heads per person per year. In a household of four, that's sixteen plastic heads a year, year after year.

The nylon bristles also wear against your teeth as you brush. We have written more about that in Microplastics in toothbrushes.

The alternative: a bamboo brush head with horsehair

There are brush heads where the stem is bamboo and the bristles are natural bristle instead of nylon. Our bamboo brush heads have horsehair bristles, attached without glue. Horsehair is a protein, not plastic, so the bristles release no microplastics.

One thing you should know: at the bottom of the head there is a small plastic connector. It is needed so the vibrations from the handle reach the brush. So the head isn't 100% plastic-free. But the bristles and the stem are, and those are the parts that wear and get thrown away.

Will it fit my electric toothbrush?

Our heads fit Philips Sonicare with the click-on fitting, for example ProtectiveClean 4100, 5100 and 6100, DiamondClean and FlexCare. A simple test: if your current head clicks straight down onto the metal shaft without twisting, it will fit.

They don't fit Oral-B, which has a round rotating head and a different fitting. Nor do they fit Philips One or Sonicare models where the head screws on. The full list is on the product page.

How to care for a head with natural bristles

  • The first time: soak the brush in warm water for 2–5 minutes so the bristles soften.
  • Before each brushing: rinse the head with water.
  • After brushing: let the head dry standing up somewhere airy. Natural bristles absorb more water than nylon and shouldn't stay wet.
  • Replace it when the bristles start to splay, roughly every three months.

Want absolutely zero plastic?

Then a manual toothbrush is the only option today. A bamboo toothbrush with wild boar bristles has a bamboo handle and boar bristles, with no plastic bristles, glue or plastic parts. We go through the difference between bristle types in Boar bristle vs nylon bristle.

Many people do both: an electric toothbrush at home with a bamboo head, and a bamboo toothbrush in the wash bag.

Frequently asked questions

Is there an electric toothbrush with no plastic at all?

We don't know of one. The handle has a battery and electronics and needs a sealed casing. You can, however, swap the brush head.

Are there bamboo brush heads for Philips Sonicare?

Yes, for Sonicare with the click-on fitting. The bristles are horsehair and the stem is bamboo. A small plastic connector at the bottom passes the vibrations on.

Is horsehair as good as nylon?

It is soft, roughly like a "soft" head. Technique matters more than material. Let the head dry between uses and replace it every three months.
