Pregnant? How to cut the plastic in your bathroom

When you're expecting a baby, many people start thinking about what they take in. Plastic is one of the things that comes up. Here is what research actually knows, what it doesn't know, and a few simple bathroom swaps for anyone who wants to play it safe.

Contents

What is known about microplastics and pregnancy?

In 2021, Italian researchers published a study in which they found microplastics in placentas for the first time. They examined six placentas and found plastic particles in four of them, on both the mother's and the baby's side. The particles were very small, less than a hundredth of a millimetre.

That sounds worrying, but it's important to know what the study doesn't say. It was small, and it shows that the particles are there, not that they harm the baby. The researchers believe the plastic enters the body through food, drink and the air we breathe. What it means for health is not yet known, and research is ongoing.

Playing it safe, where it's easy

You can't avoid plastic completely, and nobody needs to try. But while researchers don't have the answers yet, some people choose to cut down where it's easy and doesn't cost much. It's a cautious choice, not a guarantee.

The toothbrush is one of the easiest swaps. It's in your mouth twice a day and gets replaced roughly every three months. With a bamboo handle and plastic-free bristles, that's four fewer plastic brushes per person per year.

Your gums get more sensitive when you're pregnant

During pregnancy, hormones make the gums react more strongly to bacteria. They can become swollen and red and start to bleed when you brush. This is common and is called pregnancy gingivitis.

1177, Sweden's national health information service, gives three pieces of advice:

  • Brush gently with a soft toothbrush.
  • Clean between your teeth with floss or an interdental brush.
  • Contact your dentist if you have problems with your teeth or gums.

If you choose a plastic-free brush, the same applies as for everyone else: it should be soft. Hard bristles can irritate gums that are already sensitive. Among natural bristles, horsehair is a good choice here, because it is softer than wild boar bristle, which is otherwise the most common.

Plastic-free dental floss

The advice to clean between your teeth matters even more when your gums are sensitive. Ordinary dental floss is made of plastic, and some also contain PFAS, which have been linked to certain health risks. If you want to avoid plastic here too, there is silk dental floss. It is lightly waxed with plant wax so it glides smoothly between the teeth, and it is biodegradable.

Feeling sick? Rinse your mouth with water after vomiting, and wait a while before brushing your teeth. That advice also comes from 1177.
