How often should you replace your toothbrush?

Most people replace their toothbrush far too rarely. Both health authorities and research point in the same direction – roughly every three to four months – but there is a sign that matters even more than the calendar.

Contents

The basic rule: at least every 3 months

Folktandvården (Sweden's public dental service) recommends replacing your toothbrush at least every three months, preferably more often. The same rule of thumb applies across dentistry in general – roughly every three to four months. The reason is simple: the bristles wear and lose their shape, and a worn brush cleans less well.

A couple of exceptions where you may need to replace it more often: soft brushes wear faster, and children's brushes need replacing even more often, because children like to chew on the bristles.

Why it matters

When a toothbrush is too worn it loses its effect: according to Folktandvården it doesn't reach between the teeth and along the gum line as well. So you remove less plaque without noticing – and over time that raises the risk of cavities and gum inflammation. Replacing your brush in time is one of the cheapest things you can do for your oral health.

The most important sign: splayed bristles

The calendar is a rule of thumb – but look at the brush itself. As soon as the bristles start to bend outwards, splay or look ragged, it's time for a new one, even if three months haven't passed. Splayed bristles don't reach between the teeth and along the gum line as well, so your brushing becomes less effective without you noticing.

Quick test: Hold the brush in front of you. Does the head still look even and tidy? Good. Are the bristles pointing in different directions? Replace it.

Always replace it after a cold or flu

Have you been ill? Replace your brush once you're well again. Bacteria and viruses can stay on the brush, and even if the risk of reinfecting yourself is small, a fresh brush is cheap peace of mind.

Does this apply to bamboo toothbrushes too?

Yes – the same rule applies whatever the material. A bamboo toothbrush should be replaced when the bristles are worn, just like a plastic brush. The difference is what happens after you replace it: a bamboo handle can be composted instead of staying in nature for hundreds of years.
